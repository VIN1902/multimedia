# Basic
- text tool can be used as point text or paragraph text.
    - point text (pick tool and click anywhere) for when short text usage and need for layer styles.
    - paragraph text (pick tool and click + drag) for huge text and confining it withing a 'box'.

# Character panel
- reset by clicking on hamburger opition at top right.
- leading: line gap
- kerning: gap b/w two characters where you put cursor (issue in certain fonts)
- tracking: gap b/w charcters in entire text/para.
- baseline: push up or down the selected text/layer from its base position
- horizontal/vertical scale: to scale up or down

# shortcuts
- single click = add cursor there
- double click or `ctrl + shift + ->` = select word
- triple click or `shift + up/down ->` = select line
- quadruple click = select para
- `ctrl + ->` = select character

# Glyphs
- this panel shows alt version of selected text.
- varies by font and letter too.
- usefull for greek,math,science,currency,etc.

# Warp
- use text warp option after tool selection for different warp effects.

# Text on path
- just select the tool and hover over the boundary of path made by shape/pen/select&fill/etc.
- then use path selection tool to adjust start and end point.
- select the shape layer and then using type tool hover in the center of shape then the text will only appear inside the curvature of that shape.

# Font classification
1. Serif
- Features small decorative lines or "pointed feet" at the ends of the letter strokes. They look classic and are traditionally used in print (books, newspapers) for readability (e.g., Times New Roman).

1. Slab Serif
- A heavier, chunkier version of a serif font with thick, block-like, rectangular "slabs" at the letter ends. They are bold and often used for impact in headings (e.g., Rockwell).

1. Sans Serif
- Literally meaning "without serif." These fonts lack the small feet entirely. They have clean, straight lines, look modern, and are highly readable on digital screens (e.g., Arial).

1. Script
-Mimics fluid, elegant cursive handwriting or calligraphy (computerized looking). They are highly stylized and typically reserved for formal occasions, logos, or invitations (e.g., Snell Roundhand).

1. Black Letter
- Also known as Gothic or Old English. These highly ornate fonts are inspired by historical manuscript lettering from the Middle Ages, featuring thick and thin intersecting strokes. 

1. Monospace
- Every single character takes up the exact same amount of horizontal space on the line, regardless of its actual width. They mimic vintage typewriters and are primarily used for programming and coding (e.g., Courier). 

1. Handwriting
- Unlike formal scripts, these fonts mimic organic human handwriting—often resembling a pen, marker, or felt-tip aesthetic. They provide a casual, personal feel. 

1. Decorative
- Highly stylized, artistic, or novelty fonts meant to grab attention. They are meant strictly for large, eye-catching headings or branding and are generally not suited for reading large blocks of text.
